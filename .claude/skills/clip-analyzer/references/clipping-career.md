# Clipping career quick reference

## What a clipper actually sells

A clipper turns long content into short clips that earn views. You are paid for one of three things: your editing time (per clip), your judgment (which moment, which hook), or the views your reposts generate (campaigns). The judgment is the part tools cannot replace, so that is where your rate grows.

## Client intake (ask before the first clip)

1. Where is the long content and how often does new content land? (YouTube link, Drive folder, Riverside, Zoom recordings)
2. Which platforms and which accounts post the clips? Who presses publish?
3. Caption style: do they have a brand kit or do you set one?
4. Who approves clips before posting, and how fast?
5. Payment model: per clip, monthly package, or per-view campaign. Get the rate and the cap in writing.
6. Rights: written permission to edit and repost their content, and who owns the clips.
7. Hard no's: topics, guests, or words that may never appear in a clip.

## Per-clip quality checklist

- [ ] Hook lands inside the first second (spoken line, on-screen text, or both).
- [ ] One idea per clip. If you need "and also", it is two clips.
- [ ] Cut every 2 to 4 seconds or add a zoom punch so nothing sits still.
- [ ] Captions are correct, read well with sound off, and stay out of the bottom 20 percent.
- [ ] Face is centered and sharp after the 9:16 reframe. No cropped foreheads.
- [ ] Audio is leveled, silence trimmed, music under speech at low volume.
- [ ] Ends on a loop or a clear ask, never on a trailing "um".
- [ ] Export 1080x1920, 30 fps, H.264, under 60 s unless the platform target says otherwise.
- [ ] File named `client_topic_hookword_v1.mp4` so you can find it again.

## Campaign clipping cautions

- Read the campaign brief for required tags, banned edits, and minimum length. Rejected clips earn nothing.
- Payout is on verified views, often with a cap per clipper and per campaign. Plan for delays.
- Do not buy views or use bots. Accounts get banned and payouts get reversed.
- Reposting content you do not have permission for can trigger copyright takedowns. A campaign brief or a signed client agreement is your permission.

## A simple daily rhythm

1. Ingest: drop new long videos into the auto-clipper.
2. Curate: pick the 3 to 5 moments with real emotion or a real claim. Ignore the tool's score when your gut disagrees.
3. Polish: fix the hook text, restyle captions to brand, check the reframe.
4. Schedule: post at the client's best times, one clip per platform per day to start.
5. Log: a sheet with clip name, post link, posting date, and views at 24 h and 7 d. This sheet becomes your portfolio and your rate justification.
